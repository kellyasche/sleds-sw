Multivariate Confirmation Analysis: Non-College Completers in Northwest Minnesota

The primary purpose of this supplemental analysis is to examine workforce participation pathways among individuals who did not complete a postsecondary credential. Earlier sections of this analysis demonstrated that among the full Northwest Minnesota population, postsecondary attainment — particularly where and how a credential was earned — was the dominant mechanism associated with sustained regional employment. This section shifts attention to the population for whom that mechanism does not operate.

Among non-college completers, workforce participation pathways cannot be explained by credential depth, institutional sector, or graduation location. Instead, the descriptive and bivariate analyses conducted earlier identified high school career alignment (CTE), post-secondary attendance without completion, community type, and gender as the central differentiators of workforce outcomes in this population.

To strengthen confidence in those findings, a limited multivariate analysis is conducted using logistic regression. As in the full-population analysis, the goal is not causal inference or prediction, but confirmatory testing — specifically, whether the strongest bivariate relationships remain directionally consistent when key predictors are considered simultaneously.

Logistic regression is used here as a confirmatory tool rather than the primary analytic framework.


Methodology

Study Population

The analytic sample is restricted to individuals who graduated from a high school in Northwest Minnesota and did not complete a postsecondary credential. This restriction isolates workforce dynamics among high school graduates entering the labor market directly, with or without some post-secondary exposure.

Outcome Variable

The primary model predicts:

  • Meaningful employment — region (binary outcome)

The dependent variable is coded as:

  • 1 = Meaningful employment in the Northwest Minnesota region
  • 0 = All other workforce outcomes (including meaningful employment elsewhere in Minnesota, not meaningful employment, and no Minnesota employment record)

This specification maintains consistency with earlier modeling while isolating mechanisms specific to the non-college completer population.

Independent Variables

Because postsecondary completion mechanisms are not applicable in this subgroup, the model focuses on high school preparation, structural background, and local labor market context identified as important in earlier analyses. Notably, the earlier bivariate analysis found that academic readiness measures (MCA scores) predicted out-of-region mobility rather than regional attachment, while CTE engagement was the most consistent and durable predictor of regional workforce attachment across all time points.

Predictors include:

  • Demographic characteristics
    • Gender — the strongest demographic predictor, with males consistently more likely to achieve regional meaningful employment
    • RaceEthnicity — included for equity considerations despite negligible overall association
    • economic.status — included as a structural disadvantage indicator
    • SpecialEdStatus — strongest demographic predictor at year 1 in the bivariate analysis
  • High school and local context
    • Dem_Desc — the strongest high school characteristics predictor, with entirely urban non-completers showing dramatically higher disconnection rates
    • edr - regional differences.
    • avg.unemp.rate — included with the explicit caveat that its counterintuitive direction likely reflects the North Dakota cross-border employment effect
    • avg.wages.pct.state — included as a complementary county-level economic context measure
  • High school accomplishments
    • cte.achievement — the strongest and most consistent predictor of regional workforce attachment identified in the bivariate analysis
    • MCA.R — included as the primary academic readiness measure; expected to predict out-of-region rather than regional employment
    • pseo.participant — included to test whether early college exposure retains independent influence once other preparation variables are controlled
  • Post-secondary
    • attended.ps — even without completion, post-secondary attendance showed meaningful associations with workforce outcomes

Variables are selected based on:

  1. Strength of association in prior bivariate and persistence analyses
  2. Conceptual plausibility within a non-college pathway
  3. Policy relevance
  4. Avoidance of redundancy

Postsecondary completion variables are excluded by design.


Analysis Approach

This multivariate analysis mirrors the structured confirmatory framework used in the full-population models but is adapted to the non-college completer subgroup and the specific findings of the Northwest Minnesota analysis.

Time-Based Modeling Strategy

Workforce attachment evolves over time. To capture this dynamic process, models are estimated separately at:

  • 1 year after high school
  • 5 years after high school
  • 10 years after high school

This staged approach allows examination of how mechanisms shift across early, mid-term, and longer-term workforce trajectories — a distinction that proved meaningful in the bivariate analysis, where several variables (particularly economic status, special education status, and PSEO participation) showed strong early associations that faded substantially over time.

Year 1: Immediate Labor Market Entry

The 1-year model captures the initial transition into the labor market. At this stage, high school preparation and career alignment are expected to exert the strongest influence. CTE exposure emerged as the strongest high school-level predictor at year 1 in the bivariate analysis, while post-secondary attendance had its largest association at this time point due to ongoing enrollment. The year 1 model tests whether these early preparation effects hold when structural and contextual factors are considered simultaneously.

Year 5: Stabilization Phase

The 5-year model reflects early career consolidation. The bivariate analysis showed that CTE’s influence stabilized at this point while other variables — particularly economic status and PSEO participation — had largely attenuated. This model evaluates whether CTE retains its independent regional retention effect and whether gender and community type become more prominent as careers develop.

Year 10: Long-Term Embeddedness

The 10-year model captures longer-term workforce integration. The bivariate analysis suggested that MCA scores became more clearly associated with out-of-region employment by year 10, while CTE remained stable. This model tests whether local labor market context — county wages and unemployment — becomes increasingly dominant over time, and whether the North Dakota border effect continues to complicate interpretation of the unemployment rate variable.

Modeling Strategy

Within each time period, predictors are introduced in a structured sequence:

Model 1: Demographic Baseline

Includes demographic characteristics and community type:

  • RaceEthnicity
  • economic.status
  • SpecialEdStatus
  • Gender

This baseline model evaluates whether structural background independently differentiate regional workforce attachment prior to the inclusion of high school preparation variables. It also establishes whether the gender and urban/rural patterns identified in the bivariate analysis persist in a multivariate context.

Model 2: Add high school characteristics and Local Labor Market Context

Adds high school location and county-level economic context to Model 1:

  • Dem_Desc
  • edr
  • avg.wages.pct.state
  • avg.unemp.rate

Model 3: Add High School Accomplishments

Adds the following to Model 2:

  • cte.achievement
  • MCA.R
  • pseo.participant

Model 4: Add post-secondary attendance

Adds the following to Model 3:

  • attended.ps

This is the full confirmatory specification.

Evaluation Criteria

Results will be reported using:

  • Odds ratios
  • 95% confidence intervals
  • Statistical significance levels
  • Directional interpretation

Model comparison metrics (AIC and pseudo R²) will be used to assess relative improvement across specifications, but emphasis will remain on coefficient magnitude and stability across time points and model specifications.

Interpretation Framework

Interpretation will focus on:

  • Whether CTE retains independent influence across all three time points — the central confirmatory question given its dominance in the bivariate analysis
  • Whether gender and community type effects persist once preparation and economic context are controlled
  • Whether the unemployment rate’s counterintuitive direction is sustained in the multivariate context, providing further confirmation of the North Dakota border effect
  • How mechanisms shift from Year 1 to Year 10, and whether structural or contextual factors become increasingly dominant over time

Findings will be described as associations holding other included variables constant. No causal claims will be made.


Conceptual Focus

For non-college completers in Northwest Minnesota, this time-structured modeling framework evaluates three evolving mechanisms:

  1. Career alignment at labor market entry — captured primarily through CTE engagement and post-secondary attendance
  2. Structural advantage or disadvantage shaping early stability — captured through demographic characteristics and community type
  3. Local economic opportunity structures influencing long-term embeddedness — captured through county-level wage and unemployment measures, with explicit attention to the North Dakota border effect

Together, these models clarify whether sustained regional employment among non-college completers in Northwest Minnesota reflects early career alignment, structural inequality, local economic context, or a combination of all three — and whether the relative importance of these mechanisms shifts across the first decade after high school.

Multivariate Confirmation Analysis Summary — Non-College Completers in Northwest Minnesota

Multivariate Confirmation Analysis Summary — Non-College Completers in Northwest Minnesota

CTE Engagement Is the Most Consistent and Durable Associate

CTE achievement is the only high school accomplishment variable that shows a significant, stable, positive association with regional employment at all three time points and in every model specification. CTE Concentrators or Completors show odds of regional employment roughly 50-70% higher than No CTE non-completers across the full ten-year window (final model OR=1.695 at year 1, OR=1.591 at year 5, OR=1.507 at year 10), with CTE Participants showing a smaller but similarly durable positive association throughout. While the magnitude attenuates modestly over time, CTE never loses significance and never changes direction — the only variable in this analysis with that distinction. This confirms CTE as the central, policy-actionable mechanism connecting high school preparation to long-term regional workforce retention among non-completers.

Post-Secondary Attendance and Academic Proficiency Shift Direction Over Time

Two variables show genuine sign reversals across the ten-year window rather than simple attenuation. Attended post-secondary is the dominant predictor at year 1 (OR=1.692, p<.001) — the largest single association in the entire year 1 sequence — but weakens to marginal non-significance by year 5 (OR=1.059, p=.087) and crosses into negative territory by year 10 (OR=0.920, p=.064). This is not fading toward null; it is a reversal, suggesting non-completers who attempted postsecondary education without finishing may carry a modest long-term disadvantage relative to those who entered the workforce directly, despite an initial advantage.

MCA Reading shows the opposite trajectory — a monotonic strengthening rather than a reversal. It shows no independent association at year 1 (p=.075, marginal), becomes significant at year 5 (OR=0.938, p=.001), and shows its strongest effect at year 10 (OR=0.850, p<.001). This confirms academic proficiency as an increasingly powerful predictor of out-of-region mobility the further out from high school graduation one looks — the opposite pattern from attended post-secondary’s declining relevance.

Local Labor Market Context Becomes Increasingly Important Over Time

Average county unemployment rate shows the clearest monotonic pattern in the entire analysis, strengthening steadily across all three time points and every model specification: non-significant at year 1 (OR=0.994, p=.658), significant at year 5 (OR=1.028, p=.016), and most strongly significant at year 10 (OR=1.102, p<.001). This provides increasingly strong confirmation of the North Dakota cross-border employment interpretation established in the bivariate analysis — individuals with no Minnesota employment record are concentrated in lower-unemployment border counties, and this pattern becomes more pronounced, not less, as more time passes since graduation. Average wages as a percent of state, by contrast, is significant only at year 1 (OR=1.923, p=.015) and non-significant at years 5 and 10, suggesting county wage levels matter for immediate post-graduation decisions but not for longer-term attachment.

The Urban Penalty Is Structural, Persistent, and Growing

Dem_Desc’s entirely-urban penalty is the single most stable and deepening finding in this analysis. Entirely urban non-completers show significantly lower odds of regional employment relative to entirely rural non-completers at every time point and every model specification, with the penalty growing steadily more severe: OR=0.628 at year 1, OR=0.487 at year 5, OR=0.470 at year 10 (final model estimates). Unlike nearly every other variable examined, this association shows no meaningful attenuation once high school accomplishments, local labor market context, or postsecondary attendance are controlled — confirming that community type operates as an independent, structural mechanism rather than a proxy for demographic or preparation differences.

Gender Disparities Are Persistent and Structural

Females show significantly lower odds of regional employment than males at every time point and every model specification, with the gap widening slightly over time (final model OR=0.869 at year 1, OR=0.802 at year 5, OR=0.795 at year 10). The consistency and modest strengthening of this disparity, net of community type, CTE engagement, academic preparation, and local labor market context, is consistent with the bivariate interpretation that it reflects the male-dominated industry composition of the Northwest Minnesota regional economy rather than a preparation or access gap.

Racial and Ethnic Disparities Persist Net of All Variables

Racial and ethnic patterns vary considerably by group and by time point, in contrast to the more uniform demographic patterns above. Asian/PI non-completers show a significant disadvantage at every time point, growing to the largest disparity observed anywhere in this analysis by year 10 (OR=0.352). Black non-completers show a marginal association at year 1 that strengthens to a stable, significant disparity by years 5 and 10 (final model OR≈0.52 at both stages). Hispanic non-completers show a significant disparity at year 5 only, with non-significant associations at years 1 and 10. AI non-completers show no significant association at any time point or model specification — a pattern distinct from the full-population and postsecondary-completer analyses, where AI disparities were consistently among the largest observed. The persistence of Black and Asian/PI disparities net of community type, CTE engagement, academic preparation, and postsecondary attendance indicates structural barriers to regional employment that are not explained by the mechanisms examined in this analysis.

Other Structural Variables Shift Over Time

Special education status, economic status, PSEO participation, and EDR each show unstable or time-limited patterns rather than consistent trajectories. Special education status is significantly associated with lower regional employment odds at years 1 and 5 (final model OR=0.836 and OR=0.908 respectively) but becomes non-significant by year 10, and shows notable instability across model specifications within the year 10 sequence specifically — significant positive in the demographic baseline, then non-significant once academic preparation is controlled — suggesting its early disadvantage resolves over time through mechanisms only partially captured by the variables in this model. Economic status shows no significant association at years 1 or 5 but becomes significant at year 10 only once postsecondary attendance is isolated as its own term (OR=0.909, p=.040), a delayed and unexpected finding. PSEO participation shows a significant negative association at years 1 and 5 that fully resolves to non-significance by year 10. EDR shows no significant association at year 1, a significant Headwaters disadvantage at year 5 that persists only through Model 2 before attenuating, and no significant association in the final year 10 model — the weakest and least stable predictor examined, consistent with its identification as one of the two weakest bivariate predictors overall.

Overall Conclusion

Across the ten-year window examined, three findings stand out as the most robust and policy-relevant. First, CTE engagement is the only mechanism examined that shows a consistently positive, significant, and durable association with regional employment at every stage — the clearest and most actionable lever for regional workforce retention among non-college completers. Second, community type operates as a persistent and deepening structural factor independent of preparation, academic proficiency, or postsecondary exposure, with entirely urban non-completers facing a growing regional employment penalty over time. Third, the mechanisms shaping regional attachment shift substantially across the decade: early-career outcomes are dominated by postsecondary attendance status and CTE engagement, while longer-term outcomes are increasingly shaped by academic proficiency (in a direction that favors out-of-region mobility) and local labor market context, particularly the North Dakota cross-border employment pattern. This time-varying structure confirms that a single cross-sectional model would have substantially mischaracterized the mechanisms driving regional workforce attachment among non-college completers, and that policy interventions calibrated to the immediate post-graduation period — such as those targeting postsecondary transition support — may need to be paired with distinct, longer-horizon strategies addressing local economic opportunity and CTE-pathway investment to sustain regional retention over the full first decade of the workforce.
